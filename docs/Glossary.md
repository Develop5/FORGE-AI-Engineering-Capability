# Glossary

## Business Requirement

A statement of a business need, capability, outcome, or expected behavior that the system must satisfy.

A Business Requirement may be explicitly documented or identified from evidence where it is expressed indirectly, including User Stories, Acceptance Criteria, Bug Expected Results, Technical Requirements, Epics, Test Cases, or other relevant Evidence Sources.

---

## Specification

A precise, verifiable, and sufficiently detailed description of the behavior or conditions that a system must meet in order to satisfy a Business Requirement.

FORGE may produce a Specification when it is required to structure or clarify the relationship between Business Requirements and Test Cases.

A Specification does not by itself demonstrate that a Business Requirement is covered.

---

## Use Case

A description of how a user or another actor interacts with a system to achieve a particular goal.

---

## Test Case

A defined set of conditions, inputs, steps, and expected results used to verify a specific behavior or outcome of a system.

---

## Evidence

Information obtained from an Evidence Source and made available to FORGE for analysis.

Evidence may include textual content and references to the originating source.

---

## Evidence Source

A source of project information that FORGE can analyze to identify, clarify, or trace Business Requirements and Test Cases.

Examples include:

* Confluence pages
* Jira Test Cases
* Jira Bugs
* Jira User Stories
* Jira Epics
* Technical Requirements
* Acceptance Criteria
* Other relevant project documentation

---

## Requirements Discovery

The FORGE capability that analyzes available Evidence to identify and consolidate Business Requirements and identify Findings affecting those Business Requirements.

Requirements Discovery may identify:

* Business Requirements
* Conflicts
* Unresolved Dependencies
* Ambiguities
* Other problems requiring clarification

Requirements Discovery does not create Questions.

---

## Finding

A problem identified during Requirements Discovery that affects one or more Business Requirements and may require clarification.

A Finding is associated with all Business Requirements affected by the identified problem.

A Finding may represent:

* A conflict
* An ambiguity
* An unresolved dependency
* Another problem that may affect the understanding or validation of a Business Requirement

A Finding may be presented to the user through one Question during Clarification.

A Finding remains unresolved until it is resolved through clarification or Clarification ends without resolving it.

When Clarification ends with an unresolved Finding, the Finding becomes a Risk.

---

## Clarification

The FORGE capability that processes Findings and presents Questions to the user in order to resolve those Findings.

The user may:

* provide an answer;
* decide not to answer;
* decide that clarification should continue or stop.

Clarification incorporates available answers into the subsequent analysis.

When a Finding is resolved, the corresponding Question no longer exists.

If an answer reveals a new problem, that problem becomes a new Finding and may produce a new Question.

When Clarification ends with an unresolved Finding, the Finding becomes a Risk.

---

## Question

A user-facing representation of a Finding used by Clarification to request information from the user.

Each Finding produces its own Question.

A Question does not represent a persistent domain problem independently of its Finding.

When the Finding is resolved, the Question ceases to exist.

If a response reveals a new problem, the new problem is represented by a new Finding and a new Question.

---

## Traceability

The explicit relationship between Business Requirements and Test Cases, supported by the available Evidence.

Traceability allows FORGE to determine which Test Cases provide evidence for each Business Requirement.

A Business Requirement may be related to multiple Test Cases, and a Test Case may be related to multiple Business Requirements.

---

## Coverage

The percentage of Business Requirements that are covered by Test Cases.

Formula:

```text
(# of Business Requirements covered by tests) / (Total Business Requirements)
```

A Business Requirement is considered covered when it has at least one related Test Case.

Coverage is expressed as a percentage.

Insufficient evidence is not a separate Coverage category.

---

## Current Coverage

Coverage calculated from the existing Test Cases available to FORGE.

Current Coverage represents the coverage supported by the Test Cases that already exist in the available Evidence.

---

## Projected Coverage

Coverage that would result from adding the Test Cases generated by FORGE to improve Business Requirement Coverage.

Projected Coverage applies the same coverage rule as Current Coverage.

Projected Coverage is not evidence of executed Test Coverage because generated Test Cases have not been executed or validated.

---

## Coverage Result

The structured result produced by Coverage Analysis containing the Business Requirement Coverage analysis and the information required to understand the result.

The Coverage Result may include:

* Business Requirements
* Related Test Cases
* Current Coverage
* Business Requirements without identified Test Case coverage
* Risks
* Other relevant information required to explain the result

Risks and explanatory information do not participate in the Coverage calculation.

---

## Risk

A known or unresolved issue identified by FORGE that may affect the completeness, reliability, or interpretation of the analysis or its results.

A Risk must identify:

* The affected Business Requirement, when applicable
* The problem or issue identified
* The FORGE capability that identified it

A Risk may also include:

* The unanswered or unresolved Question that led to it, when applicable
* Relevant supporting information or references

Every Risk originates from a Finding or from another explicitly identified issue produced by a FORGE capability.

Risks may include:

* Unresolved Findings after Clarification
* Insufficient evidence
* Functional inconsistencies
* Insufficient information to generate a required Test Case
* Other issues that may affect the interpretation or reliability of the analysis

A Business Requirement without identified Test Case coverage is not automatically a Risk.

Risks may be identified by different FORGE capabilities and are carried forward to subsequent analysis and reporting.

Risks do not participate in the Coverage calculation.

---

## Requirement Issues

Problems identified during Requirements Discovery or Clarification that may affect the understanding or validation of Business Requirements.

Requirement Issues include:

* Ambiguities
* Conflicts among requirements
* Contradictions among requirements or within a requirement
* Circular dependencies
